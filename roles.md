# Roles

An organization or team isn't permanently "the owner" of an initiative — ownership is a role attached to a specific concept at a specific lifecycle stage, and it moves. This mirrors the point CommonGood Atlas makes about grantmaking organizations not having one fixed identity (funder, grantee, fiscal sponsor): the same team can hold different roles on different initiatives, and different roles on the *same* initiative as it moves through its lifecycle.

## Typical ownership by function, in a 5-function operating model

| Function | Typically owns |
|---|---|
| Business (domain leader) | [Business Problem](./concepts/business-problem.md), [Value Realization](./concepts/value-realization.md) target |
| Operations | [Workflow](./concepts/workflow.md) design, [Field Readiness](./concepts/field-readiness.md) |
| Technology / Data | [Execution Plan](./concepts/execution-plan.md) |
| Domain specialists (e.g., clinical, technical, regulatory) | Workflow design wherever specialized systems or expertise are touched |
| Field leadership | [Adoption](./concepts/adoption.md), and feeding field feedback back into Workflow revisions |

## Rule

The handoff points between functions are exactly where accountability tends to go missing if they aren't made explicit. A Workflow that's fully designed by Operations but never formally handed to Technology for an Execution Plan, or an Execution Plan that's complete but never formally handed to Field leadership to drive Adoption, is where initiatives stall without anyone being clearly responsible for the stall.
